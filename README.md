## Understand the examination and the licensing goal

NJ-Life-Producer is the subject label used here for New Jersey Life Producer preparation. The official PSI outline is titled Life Producer & Instructor Examination. This article concerns the Producer examination, not the separate Instructor passing standard.

The official materials record:

- Testing administrator: PSI Services LLC.
- Licensing authority: New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance.
- Scored examination items: 83.
- Time allowed: 3.5 hours.
- Producer passing standard: 70% correct.
- Examination fee: $38, separate from license application fees.

PSI states that an additional 5 to 19 experimental questions may appear without contributing to the score. Review current PSI instructions when planning your appointment.

Passing the exam does not itself issue a license. Candidates must complete the applicable licensing process and receive authorization from the state.

## Organize the six subject areas

The linked official outline assigns nine items to policy types. It names whole life, interest-sensitive products, term insurance, annuities, and combination plans such as joint and survivorship life.

Riders, provisions, options, and exclusions account for 21 items. Study beneficiary designations, ownership, premium payment, reinstatement, loans, nonforfeiture, dividends, settlement options, and the other named contract subjects.

Application completion, underwriting, and policy delivery account for 12 items. Review signatures, application changes, premiums and receipts, disclosures, insurable interest, risk classification, and explaining the policy at delivery.

Taxes, retirement, and other insurance concepts account for eight items. The scope includes group life, retirement plans, business insurance, tax treatment, modified endowment contracts, and living benefits.

The remaining sections address New Jersey rules: 25 items for provisions common to major insurance lines and eight for life-specific rules. Treat these as their own study areas instead of assuming general product knowledge covers them.

## Build comparisons that answer a clear question

For products, create a private study comparison using the categories in your course. Explain which feature separates one product from another. Confirm the explanation before using it as a revision note.

For contract topics, compare terms that you tend to confuse. Beneficiary categories, policy ownership, payment options, and riders deserve separate attention. Write the difference in your own words rather than copying a paragraph without testing recall.

For underwriting, follow the application through the activities named in the outline. Identify what each stage asks you to understand. Keep the exercise educational; it is not advice for a particular applicant or insurance purchase.

The best comparison is narrow enough to check. If a note contains several uncertain statements, split it into smaller questions.

## Give New Jersey rules a distinct review routine

The common-law section includes regulatory jurisdiction, definitions, licensing, trade practices, guaranty associations, and ethics. Life-specific topics include marketing, replacement, disclosures, advertising, contracts, suitability, and appropriate professional titles.

Use approved education and current state material to study the applicable rules. The official outline identifies subjects, but its older references should not be treated as a substitute for checking current requirements.

Keep general insurance definitions separate from New Jersey-specific obligations in your notes. That makes it easier to see whether a practice error concerns the concept or the state's rule.

Do not use an examination article to decide an individual's legal eligibility or a customer's financial needs.
